The opportunity

Solve a specific business problem with the right model.

Build a model around a defined business task rather than starting with an algorithm. We assess data quality and feasibility, compare a simple baseline with suitable approaches, and evaluate results against the errors that matter to your workflow. The final scope includes how predictions will be used and maintained.

In practice

How a model learns from past examples

Machine learning, or ML, uses patterns in examples to make a prediction on a new case. A team might use past records to sort incoming requests into likely categories before someone checks the result.

Define a specific prediction

We agree what the model should predict and what the team will do with the result. A clear task gives us something to test.

Prepare examples and a comparison

We review the data for gaps and hold back some examples for testing. We compare the model with a simpler method that is already available.

Decide how to use the result

If the model is useful, we plan how it reaches the team and what happens when its output is uncertain or the work changes.

What to share with us

Share example records, what happened in each case, and which mistakes would matter most to your team.

Rank items for a defined decision

Develop a scoring or ranking approach for a specific review workflow, using inputs that are available when the decision must be made.

Before we start

Questions worth answering.

Do we need a custom model?

Not always. Rules, an existing model or a simpler workflow may meet the goal. The feasibility stage helps determine whether custom training is justified.

Who owns the model and code?

Ownership, licences and handover are defined in the project agreement, including any restrictions from datasets, pretrained models or third-party services.

Can you work with our existing dataset?

Yes. We first check its relevance, quality, labelling and permitted use before agreeing what additional preparation is needed.

Can you improve a model we already have?

We can review its evaluation, data and failure cases, then test focused improvements. The first step is checking that the reported baseline is reproducible and relevant.

Will a more complex model always work better?

No. Simpler approaches can be easier to maintain and may perform as well for the task. We compare complexity against measurable benefit and operating constraints.
