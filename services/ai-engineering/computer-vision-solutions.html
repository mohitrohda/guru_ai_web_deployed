The opportunity

Build software that understands what it sees.

Turn camera images and video into information your team can act on. We start with the environment, objects and decisions involved, then evaluate whether a vision model is a suitable approach. A practical solution includes image capture, clear operating limits and a way to handle uncertain results.

In practice

What a camera-based check can look like

Computer vision uses images or video to find objects, count items, or flag a visible issue. For example, an inspection tool could highlight a part that may have a surface mark so a technician can check it.

Capture examples from the real setting

We review the camera angle, lighting, and changes in the items being observed. The examples should reflect the conditions where the tool will run.

Choose the decision it supports

We agree whether the tool should count, locate, or flag something, and how people will use that output.

Check errors before rollout

We can test both things the tool misses and things it marks incorrectly. Your team can decide when review is required.

What to share with us

Bring sample images of normal and unusual cases, plus a description of how people check them today.

Production quality

Flag visible product or packaging issues

A camera captures products at a defined point in the process. A vision model flags agreed visible conditions for a reviewer or a downstream operational workflow.

Design considerationThe pilot should cover lighting changes, product variation and difficult examples. Acceptance criteria include both missed issues and unnecessary alerts.

Counting workflows

Count objects at a controlled checkpoint

Use a suitable camera view to count agreed items moving through a defined area. The system can present observations alongside the relevant image or video segment.

Design considerationOcclusion, overlap and changes in direction affect counting. The capture setup and test conditions are specified before treating counts as operational records.

Operational review

Find visual events worth checking

Define a limited set of events that a team wants to review, then route candidate observations into an organised queue rather than asking people to inspect every frame.

Design considerationAlert frequency, access and retention should reflect the actual operational need. The system's output remains bounded by the camera view and tested conditions.

Before we start

Questions worth answering.

Will it work with our existing cameras?

Possibly. We assess resolution, frame rate, viewing angle, lighting and access to the video stream before confirming suitability.

Can it run on an edge device?

We can evaluate edge and server deployment based on hardware, latency, connectivity and model requirements.

Can it replace manual quality inspection?

That depends on the task and validation evidence. A pilot should establish which decisions can be automated and which require human review.

Can a model trained elsewhere work immediately in our environment?

It may provide a starting point, but cameras, lighting, products and viewing angles can change performance. Evaluation on your representative data is necessary.

How are uncertain detections handled?

We can define confidence thresholds and review paths, then evaluate the resulting trade-off between missed events and false alarms. A confidence score is not a guarantee of correctness.
