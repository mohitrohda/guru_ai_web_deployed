Customer records · Buying guide

A first CRM for a small business

A CRM gives your team one place to see who asked about your services, who is responsible for replying, and what should happen next. Start with a real enquiry your team has already handled.

By GURU AI Technologies, Pune · Updated 6 November 2026

Follow one enquiry from start to finish

Imagine a customer sends a message after seeing your website. Someone replies, sends a quote, and plans to follow up next week. The record should show the customer’s name, the request, who owns it, the last contact, and the next action.

Keep the stages simple. A small team might use New, Talking, Quote sent, and Closed. Give each stage a clear meaning, so people update records the same way.

Decide what to automate

A reminder for an overdue reply is a good first rule. Automating every message can create confusion if a customer needs a person or has already replied. Decide where a team member should check the conversation before a message goes out.

The CRM walkthrough uses sample data to show how an enquiry can move from arrival to a follow-up. You can start with a simpler version and add rules after the team uses it.

Work out when a spreadsheet is enough

A shared spreadsheet can be adequate while one or two people own the enquiries and can keep the list current. A CRM becomes more useful when reminders are missed, different people contact the same lead, or you cannot tell which requests are open.

Bring the right example to a first discussion

Show the columns you already use and bring a short example with personal information removed. Agree on which records to import, who can see them, and which task the first version should make easier.

What to check

  • A sample enquiry with private information removed
  • Current sales stages and owners
  • Where messages and quotes are kept now
  • One follow-up rule you want to test first
