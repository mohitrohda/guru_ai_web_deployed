What goes into a website quote?

Two business websites can look similar and take very different amounts of work. The quickest way to get a useful quote is to describe what a customer should be able to do when they visit.

By GURU AI Technologies, Pune · Updated 6 November 2026

Start with the customer’s next step

A service business may only need visitors to find its location, check what it offers, and ask a question on WhatsApp. A business that takes reservations needs to decide who owns the calendar, what counts as a confirmed booking, and what customers see if no times are available.

List your main services and pick one action for the first version. If you need a large catalogue or customers need to pay online, say so early. These change the work even when the homepage looks simple.

Find out what the quote covers

Ask who writes the copy, prepares photos, buys the domain, and adds products or service pages. Check whether the quote includes forms, basic search setup, mobile testing, launch, and help updating the site later.

Separate the cost of building the site from recurring costs such as hosting, domain renewal, booking tools, or ongoing maintenance. A clear proposal tells you which costs come from the studio and which go to another provider.

Plan the timeline around the material you have

Our usual range for a business website is one to three weeks. That is a starting point for the conversation. The schedule depends on how soon you can share the content, approve designs, and provide access to tools such as your domain or calendar.

In our Accusize project, the public site presented a manufacturer’s catalogue, while the internal ERP and its calibration module handled records the staff needed. It is a useful example of why a website quote should separate what customers need to see from what staff need to manage.

Send a short brief

Share what you do, who visits your site, and the one thing you want those visitors to do. Add links to your current website or listings, note any deadline, and mention whether your copy and photos are ready. We can use that to discuss what belongs in the first version and what can wait.

Compare project options and request a quote.

What to bring

  • Main services and the action visitors should take
  • Existing site, domain, logo and photos
  • Any enquiry, booking or payment tools
  • Who approves content and when it is needed
